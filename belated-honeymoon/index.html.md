# Belated Honeymoons: Why More Couples Take a Minimoon Now and the Big Trip Later

A belated honeymoon is the main honeymoon you take weeks or months after the wedding. You can go straight home after the reception, or take a short minimoon first and save the bigger trip for a time when both of you can enjoy it. Your first anniversary is one possible date. So is an ordinary week when you can get time off together.

Waiting adds a second date to the calendar, which can feel freeing or like one more thing to organize. Start by looking at the [trade-offs of taking two trips](#trade-offs). If staying close to home sounds appealing, read the [minimoon planning guide](#minimoon) before booking anything.

## Minimoon, belated honeymoon and pre-moon

A minimoon is a brief getaway around the wedding, often a couple of nights close to home. It can be your only honeymoon or a small first trip. A belated honeymoon is the main trip you plan to take after the dust has settled. A pre-moon happens before the wedding. Think of it as time together away from the guest list and seating chart, especially if the weeks after the ceremony are already spoken for.

Travel agency [Fora’s wedding and honeymoon trend report](https://www.foratravel.com/the-journal/fora-wedding-and-honeymoon-report-2026) says 59% of couples are choosing a minimoon and putting the main trip off. The report draws on Fora’s booking data and a February advisor survey. Its summary gives no basis for treating that percentage as a national estimate. Separately, 64% of the agency’s advisors reported clients choosing off-season or shoulder-season travel. That figure describes advisor responses, so it answers a different question from the percentage of couples.

## Why couples choose to wait

By the time your guests go home, you may want a quiet breakfast far more than you want to make a tight connection. In [Bustle’s first-person account](https://www.bustle.com/life/belated-honeymoons-trend-planning), Gabrielle Bondi describes putting the honeymoon off until the couple’s first anniversary. Waiting took one planning job off the pre-wedding list and gave the trip its own attention later.

There are practical reasons to wait, too. One of you might have already used vacation days for the wedding. Your destination may have its best weather in a different season. Or you may want to see how the final wedding bills land before working out the trip budget. If you are still building that budget, go through these [often-overlooked wedding expenses](https://wedding.allwomenstalk.com/critical-wedding-expenses-you-cant-forget-to-budget-for/) first.

One drawback of two trips is asking for time off twice. A minimoon can also quietly grow into the same kind of project you were trying to postpone. Give the short trip a concrete limit in advance. If a longer trip keeps getting bumped by work or family plans, pick a month together and agree when you will revisit the calendar.

## How long can you wait?

Choose an approximate window around the thing you can control least. If that is work, look for the first overlap in your leave calendars. If it is the destination, compare seasons and the activities that made you want to go in the first place. If it is the budget, put a realistic amount of time between the wedding and the point when you expect to make deposits.

A first anniversary makes for a ready-made occasion. An ordinary week may suit your time off better. Share a calendar entry for the first booking decision and agree on a backup window. That makes the plan more concrete while leaving room to change it.

## Plan a minimoon that feels like a break

Choose an easy drive or train ride and a place where you can enjoy the day without a full itinerary. Book the lodging and one thing you both want, whether that is a good dinner, a few hours by the water or a late checkout. Leave the rest of the day open. Our [romantic getaway planning guide](https://travel.allwomenstalk.com/tips-on-how-to-plan-a-perfect-romantic-getaway/) has ideas for making a short trip feel intentional.

If you are leaving the day after the reception, pack before wedding week and check when you can get into your room. Give yourselves time to say goodbye to guests or retrieve anything from the venue. Even a one-night stay can feel more relaxed when the handoff is sorted out.

## Make the later trip feel like your honeymoon

Start with what you want to share on the trip. Each of you can choose one experience to build around and one to leave optional. Look through these [dream honeymoon destinations](https://wedding.allwomenstalk.com/places-to-go-for-your-dream-honeymoon/) or [less expected honeymoon ideas](https://wedding.allwomenstalk.com/offbeat-honeymoon-destinations/) if you are still deciding where to go. Telling the hotel you are celebrating can help explain a dinner or room request. Plan on paying for the arrangements you want.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a belated honeymoon?

It is the main honeymoon taken after a gap following the wedding, often a few weeks, several months or around the first anniversary.

### What is a minimoon?

A minimoon is a short celebratory trip around the wedding. It can be the whole honeymoon or a small getaway before a longer trip.

### Is it OK to delay your honeymoon?

Yes. Match the date to your time off, budget and the trip you want. The wedding date gives you an occasion to celebrate, while the travel date can fit your circumstances.

### How long after the wedding can you take a honeymoon?

There is no fixed cutoff. Pick a month that works for both of you and put a booking check-in on the calendar so you have a date to work toward.

### What are the main pros and cons?

Waiting can give you more time to plan and more choice of seasons. Taking two trips adds another set of expenses and dates, and a main trip without a date can be easy to postpone again.
